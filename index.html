🧠 Conquering the 700+ Slides Dilemma: Top-1% MED II & Step 1 Strategy
Evidence-Based Protocol

1. Why Reading 700+ Slides Passively Fails

Trying to re-read 700+ lecture slides in the final 2–3 days before an exam triggers fluency illusion (recognizing text visually without being able to retrieve it under exam pressure). Worse, crowding all 10 UWorld blocks (400 questions) into the final 72 hours means ~130+ Qs/day, leaving zero cognitive bandwidth to actually learn from explanations.

2. The 80/20 "Slide Delta" Method

80%+ of your UOB basic science exam is already covered by B&B + First Aid 2026 + Anki + UWorld. Instead of reading 700 slides linearly, do a 15-minute "Slide Delta Pass" on the day of each Dekweneh lecture: skim the professor's slides ONLY for what is NOT in First Aid (e.g., a local epidemiological stat, a specific imaging slide from Dr. Haddad/Kheir, or a red-bolded clinical pearls table from Dr. Atallah/Saliba) and tag those 5–10 slides into a single "Exam Delta Deck."

3. Distributed UWorld Pacing (40–80 Qs/Day)

Start UWorld 6–7 days before the exam (1 block/day while finishing the last B&B videos, then 2 blocks/day during the 4-day dedicated window). For every wrong question, write a 1-line "Pivot Rule" in First Aid (what clinical clue would have made you pick B over A?). In the final 24 hours before the exam, review ONLY your annotated First Aid pages, your UWorld Incorrects, and your 30-slide "Professor Delta" file.

🚀 Step-by-Step Guide: Host on GitHub Pages & Install on iPhone with Permanent Device Storage
2-Minute Setup

Step 1: Create Your Free GitHub Pages Repository

  1. Log in to github.com and click the + icon (top right) → New repository.
  2. Name the repository med2-planner (or ali-med2), set it to Public, check Add a README file, and click Create repository.

Step 2: Upload This File as index.html

  1. Download ali_med2_command_center.html to your computer and rename it to index.html.
  2. Inside your new GitHub repository, click Add file → Upload files.
  3. Drag and drop your renamed index.html file and click the green Commit changes button at the bottom.

Step 3: Turn On GitHub Pages

  1. Click Settings (top tab of your repository) → click Pages in the left sidebar.
  2. Under Build and deployment → Branch, change None to main (keep folder as / (root)) and click Save.
  3. Wait ~60 seconds and refresh that page: GitHub will display your live URL: https://<your-username>.github.io/med2-planner/!

Step 4: Install on iPhone Safari ("Add to Home Screen")

  1. Open your live https://<your-username>.github.io/med2-planner/ link in Safari on your iPhone.
  2. Tap the Share icon (square with upward arrow) at the bottom of Safari.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen → tap Add.
  4. Your custom golden Rod of Asclepius & Blaugrana ECG icon will now sit on your iPhone Home Screen and launch full-screen without Safari's address bar!

🔒 How Your Data Stays Permanently Saved (Zero Redoing!)

  • Dual-Engine On-Device Storage: Every time you add/edit an event or check off a B&B video, UWorld block, or Anki block, the app writes your state simultaneously to localStorage AND IndexedDB (with navigator.storage.persist() enabled).
  • Crucial iPhone Tip: On iOS, Apple gives your Home Screen App icon its own dedicated storage vault separate from regular Safari tabs. Once you tap Add to Home Screen, always open the app from your Home Screen icon so you are always inside the exact same persistent database.
  • Moving Data Between Laptop & iPhone: Since GitHub Pages is a static host (no external server database), if you ever want to copy your latest edits from your laptop to your iPhone (or keep a cloud backup in iCloud Drive), just tap 💾 Export Backup (.json) on one device and 📂 Import Backup (.json) on the other!
